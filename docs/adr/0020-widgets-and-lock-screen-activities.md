# ADR-0020 — Home-screen widgets and lock-screen activities

- **Date:** 2026-10-23
- **Status:** accepted
- **Deciders:** owner (delegated: "decide rather than ask")

## Context

Unfurl's person tracks habits, tasks, training, meals and money in one app.
The apps they would otherwise use each put their one number on the home
screen: Streaks and Routine Flow show today's habits and let you tick one off,
MacroFactor shows calories and protein left, Hevy and Strong show the running
workout with its rest timer on the lock screen, budgeting apps show what is
safe to spend. Unfurl had none of these, and a routine or workout in progress
disappeared the moment the phone locked.

Constraints that shape the choice:

- **Local-first.** The stores live on the phone (`shared/prototype/*`, synced by
  `mobile/src/live`). A widget must work offline, from data on the device.
- **Free Apple team.** The owner signs with a personal team until the paid
  account is approved. A personal team **cannot sign App Groups** (the usual
  way an app shares data with its widget extension) or the push entitlement.
  Its provisioning profile does allow keychain groups under its own team prefix
  (`N2Y99Q8A79.*`).
- **Actions must land where in-app taps land.** A tap on the lock screen has to
  call the same store action as the in-app button, so it reaches the outbox
  and syncs like any other change, including when the app had been killed.
- **Continuous Native Generation.** `ios/` and `android/` are generated by
  `expo prebuild` and not committed, so all native code ships through config
  plugins or local Expo modules.

## Decision

### Shared pieces (JS)

- `mobile/src/surfaces/snapshot.ts` builds one **widget snapshot** (JSON) from
  the stores with the same rules Today uses: day ring, Life Score and streak
  (from the dashboard hooks, so the server's numbers win in live mode), due
  habits with streaks, macros left, overdue and next tasks, safe-to-spend, and
  the running activity. It respects the privacy toggle and modules switched
  off. `SurfacesBridge` (mounted in the signed-in layout) writes it, debounced,
  whenever it changes and at the day turn (4:00).
- `mobile/src/surfaces/live-state.ts` builds the **live state** of the running
  routine, habit timer or workout: current step or set, the next eight,
  progress, pause state, and absolute end times so native timers count on their
  own. Its key ignores the clock, so the app pushes an update only when
  something the person sees changes (ActivityKit rations updates).
- `mobile/src/surfaces/actions.ts` applies **queued taps**. Each carries the
  step or set the person saw; a tap whose step the app already left, or one
  delivered twice, is dropped instead of advancing the wrong step. Each maps to
  the existing domain action (`stepDone`, `stepSkip`, `toggleRunPause`,
  `pauseLive`/`resumeLive`, `finishTimer`, `toggleSet`, `skipRest`,
  `adjustRest`, `finishWorkout`, `checkHabit`).
- `mobile/modules/unfurl-surfaces` is a local Expo module: `setSnapshot`,
  `setLive`, `drainActions` and an `onAction` event.

### iOS: WidgetKit + ActivityKit through `@bacons/apple-targets`

- `mobile/targets/widget` is a widget extension generated at prebuild by
  `@bacons/apple-targets` (chosen over `expo-widgets`, which is iOS-only, renders
  through a separate JS runtime, and reports widget presses to the app only
  while it is running). Widgets are SwiftUI: Today, Habits, Macros, Up next,
  Money, in small/medium (Habits also large) and lock-screen accessory sizes,
  plus the Live Activity (lock screen and Dynamic Island).
- **Data channel: a shared keychain item**, access group
  `$(AppIdentifierPrefix)com.schadenkai.personal-sop.surfaces`, which a free
  team can sign. The app's own group stays first in its list so existing
  secure-store items keep their default group. When the account is paid, an
  App Group can replace it inside `SharedStore` alone.
- **Buttons are App Intents declared as `LiveActivityIntent`** in
  `targets/widget/_shared/UnfurlShared.swift`, which apple-targets compiles into
  the app target too. The system runs a `LiveActivityIntent` in the app's
  process, launching it in the background when it was killed. The intent queues
  the tap in the shared store, moves the Live Activity on at once (the same
  transition rules the app applies), and posts a Darwin notification; the
  module turns that into `onAction` and JS drains the queue. On a cold start the
  app drains on mount. The Habits widget's tick is also a `LiveActivityIntent`
  for the same reason.
- Updates are local (`Activity.update`), no push token: push needs the paid
  account and is not required while the app can run in the background.
- `plugins/free-apple-team.js`: with `UNFURL_FREE_APPLE_TEAM=1` at prebuild it
  removes the `aps-environment` entitlement that `expo-notifications` adds, the
  one entitlement a free team cannot sign. Every other entitlement stays.

### Android: RemoteViews widgets + an ongoing notification, in the module

- Widgets are `AppWidgetProvider`s with RemoteViews layouts inside the local
  module (no extra library; Glance would add the Compose compiler to the build
  for five small layouts). They read the snapshot from SharedPreferences, pick
  a small or wide layout by size on Android 12+, and follow light/dark through
  `values-night` colours generated from the design tokens.
- The running activity is an **ongoing notification** (low importance, silent)
  with a chronometer counting the rest, step or elapsed time, a progress bar,
  and up to three action buttons. Not a foreground service: nothing has to
  keep running between taps, and a service type would add Play policy
  declarations for no gain.
- Buttons and the widget tick are `PendingIntent` broadcasts to
  `SurfaceActionReceiver`, which queues the tap, updates the notification or
  widget at once, and then either emits `onAction` (JS running) or starts
  `UnfurlSurfacesTaskService`, a headless JS task that applies the queue to the
  stores straight away. The sync engine pushes those changes on its next run, as
  it diffs every store against the last state it sent.

## Constitution clause deviated from

None. The mobile app's stack choices (Expo, CNG) stand; this adds a local Expo
module and one config-plugin dependency (`@bacons/apple-targets`).

## Consequences

**Positive:**

- Five widgets on both platforms and a controllable lock-screen activity, from
  data already on the phone; nothing needs the network or the paid account.
- One code path for every tap: in-app, lock screen, Dynamic Island,
  notification, widget.

**Negative:**

- The Swift and Kotlin transition rules duplicate a small part of the domain
  logic (advance a step, start rest) for the instant feedback; the app's own
  result replaces them as soon as it runs.
- A tap applied late is timed when the app applies it for routine steps (the
  domain actions read the clock); finishing a timer or a workout uses the tap's
  time. In practice iOS and Android both apply within seconds.
- Free-team builds have no remote push; the 7-day profile also limits how many
  new bundle ids (the widget extension adds one) the team can register a week.
